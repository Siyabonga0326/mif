About Us

Who we are

Mzurayza Ingwenya Foundation was established in 2019 and formally became a Non-Profit Organisation in 2025. Founded under the leadership of Mzuvele Mkhize — known in the arts community as Mzurayza — the organisation operates in KwaMaphumulo and surrounding rural communities in KwaZulu-Natal.

Our vision is to be a creative space where every person, mainly youth from rural areas, has access to skill development, sport, arts and culture. Our mission is to empower young people through the promotion and development of education, sport, arts and culture.

Our vision

A creative space where every person — mainly youth from rural areas — has access to skill development, sport, arts and culture.

Our mission

To empower young people through the promotion and development of education, sport, arts and culture across rural communities.

Who we serve

Youth, women, people living with disability, and the broader rural community of KwaMaphumulo and the iLembe district.

Where we operate

KwaMaphumulo, KwaZulu-Natal — a predominantly rural area under the iLembe district municipality.

What We Do

Our programs & activities

We take a holistic approach to youth development — combining sport, arts, culture and education in a way that keeps young people engaged, inspired and protected from the social challenges facing rural communities.

Sport

Youth School Championship

Annual tournament established in 2015, now bringing together 10 secondary and 9 primary schools across Maphumulo in soccer, netball, and arts competitions each Youth Day (16 June).

Arts & Culture

Maphumulo Ikhono Showcase

A Heritage Day talent showcase celebrating local artists across music, dance, fine art, poetry, and performance. Meaning "Maphumulo, showcase your talent."

Community

Maqadini Tournament

A community soccer tournament that creates spaces for sport and healthy competition in the rural areas of Maphumulo.

Education

Skill development

Workshops, career guidance and counselling that help educationally disadvantaged young people enter the creative industry or find employment.

Partnerships

Maphumulo Festival

A collaborative arts festival with Mzutainment featuring workshops, exhibitions, and arts performances to expose youth to cultural diversity.

Heritage

Cultural preservation

Training in visual arts, drama, dance, indigenous arts and music — preserving heritage while creating pathways for rural youth into the creative sector.

Our impact

Why this work matters

Maphumulo faces significant challenges — crime, drug abuse, gender-based violence, and teenage pregnancy. Our programmes are a proactive strategy. When young people have meaningful platforms to showcase their talents, they are less vulnerable and more hopeful.
